What moves a break fee

1

How far rates have fallen

The fee reflects the gap between the rate you fixed at and the rate the bank could lend the same money at today. No fall usually means little or no fee.

2

How much is fixed

The fee scales with the balance. If only part of your loan is on the fixed rate you want to break, enter that part.

3

How long is left

Eighteen months left costs roughly three times as much as six months left on the same loan and rates.

4

Your bank's own method

Banks must explain how they calculate the charge. The exact figure changes daily, so a quote is only good for a short time.

How mortgage break fees work in NZ

A break fee, which banks call an early repayment charge or early repayment recovery, applies when you repay or refinance a fixed-rate loan before the fixed term ends. It can also apply to a large lump-sum repayment. The charge compensates the bank when interest rates have fallen since you fixed, because it can only re-lend your money at a lower rate.

How the fee is calculated

Early repayment charges on home loans are regulated by the Credit Contracts and Consumer Finance Act 2003. The regulations set out a formula lenders may use. A lender can use its own method instead, as long as the result is a reasonable estimate of its loss, and it has to tell you which method it uses and how the charge is worked out. The Banking Ombudsman notes that many banks base the charge on the difference between wholesale interest rates when you fixed and when you repay.

You can't look up the wholesale rates a bank uses, so this calculator works from advertised rates: your fixed rate, less the bank's current rate for a term close to the time you have left, multiplied by the balance and the time remaining. On $500,000 fixed at 6.49% with 18 months to run, and a current 18-month rate of 4.99%, that comes to about $11,250. Treat it as a guide to the size of the number, then get the real one from your bank.

When there's no break fee

  • Rates are the same or higher than when you fixed. The bank hasn't lost anything by getting its money back early.
  • Your loan is floating or on revolving credit. Break fees apply only to fixed rates.
  • You wait for the fixed term to end. Refinancing on the expiry date avoids the fee entirely.
  • You stay within your bank's extra repayment allowance. Most banks let you pay a set amount off a fixed loan each year without a charge, and the amount differs by bank.

Is breaking worth it?

Breaking pays only when what you gain is clearly more than what it costs. On the gain side are the interest you save at the lower rate until your old term would have ended, and any cashback from a new lender. On the cost side are the break fee, legal fees if you change lender, and any cashback you have to repay to your current bank. Our cashback clawback calculator estimates that last one.

Because the fee is designed to recover the bank's loss, the interest you save by breaking is often close to the fee you pay. That's why the result above is frequently near zero. Breaking tends to make sense when you need to restructure or sell anyway, or when a new lender's offer tips the balance.

Getting your bank's figure

Ask your bank for a break cost quote in writing. It's free, and it's the only figure that counts. For the full picture, read our guide to breaking a fixed mortgage in NZ, or run a switch through the refinance savings calculator.

Common questions

How much is a mortgage break fee in NZ?

It depends on how far interest rates have fallen since you fixed, how much is fixed and how long is left on the term. If rates are the same or higher than when you fixed, the fee is usually small or nil. Only your bank can give the exact figure.

How do I find out my exact break fee?

Ask your bank for a break cost quote in writing. Quotes are free. The figure changes daily with interest rates, so a quote is only valid for a short time.

Can a break fee be waived or negotiated?

You can ask, but the bank doesn't have to agree. The Banking Ombudsman's guidance is to assume the charge will apply in full.

Does a break fee apply when I sell my house?

It can, if you repay a fixed-rate loan before the term ends. If you're buying again, ask your bank whether the loan can be moved to the new property, which may avoid the charge.
