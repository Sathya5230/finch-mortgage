Local knowledge

Home loans and refinancing across Otago

Otago runs from the Waitaki River to the Catlins and inland to the lakes and mountains of Queenstown and Wānaka. Dunedin is the main city, Central Otago is orchard and vineyard country, and the Queenstown Lakes district has some of the highest property prices in New Zealand.

That spread means very different borrowing in different towns: large, carefully structured loans in Wānaka and Arrowtown, seasonal orchard incomes in Cromwell, Alexandra and Roxburgh, and affordable older homes in Ōamaru, Balclutha and Milton. We compare 23 lenders by phone, video and secure online document upload.

What lenders look at in Otago

Large loans in the Queenstown Lakes

In Wānaka and Arrowtown, loans are bigger and debt-to-income limits bite sooner. Lender choice and loan structure make a real difference at these prices.

Orchard and seasonal incomes

Cherries, apricots and pinot noir drive Central Otago. Seasonal and self-employed incomes need presenting the way each lender assesses them.

Cribs and holiday homes

From Naseby to Wānaka, holiday homes are usually financed as investment-type loans, and lenders differ on counting short-stay income.

Older homes and heritage

Ōamaru's Victorian villas and the stone cottages of Clyde and Arrowtown are lendable, but insurance can cost more, so get a quote before you go unconditional.

Refinancing in Otago: five checks first

  1. Start four to six weeks before your fixed term ends. There's no break fee at the end of a fixed term, so that's the cleanest moment to switch or renegotiate.
  2. Know your break fee if you're mid-term. Your bank can quote it. We only recommend breaking a fixed rate when the saving clearly beats the cost.
  3. Check for a cashback clawback. If your current lender paid you a cash contribution in the last few years, some or all of it may have to be repaid if you leave.
  4. Count the switching costs. Expect legal fees for the new mortgage and sometimes a valuation. Some lenders contribute towards these.
  5. Compare your own bank's offer too. Sometimes the best result is a better deal from the lender you're already with, and we'll tell you if that's the case.
