NZ Mortgage Market

What Moves NZ Mortgage Rates
and Lending Rules.

Reviewed: November 2026

Rates and prices change every week, and we don't publish numbers we can't keep current. This page explains the settings behind them, what each means for your borrowing, and where to find today's official figures.

The Settings

Six things that decide
what you can borrow.

Settings are as at November 2026. The Reserve Bank and the Government change them from time to time, so confirm the current position with the sources further down.

The Official Cash Rate

The Reserve Bank sets the OCR several times a year. Floating mortgage rates usually follow it within days. Fixed rates move earlier, on what markets expect the OCR to do next, so they can fall before a cut or rise before a hike.

How the OCR affects your mortgage →

Deposit rules (LVR)

The Reserve Bank limits how much low-deposit lending banks can do. Most owner-occupier loans need a 20% deposit and most loans on existing rentals need about 30%, with a limited allowance above those levels that was widened on 1 December 2025. New builds are exempt.

How LVR works →

Debt-to-income limits

Banks can make only a small share of new loans to owner-occupiers borrowing more than six times their income, or investors borrowing more than seven times. New builds are exempt. For many buyers this now sets the ceiling, not the deposit.

DTI limits explained →

Each bank's test rate

Lenders check you could afford the loan at a rate above the one you'll pay. Each lender sets its own test rate and changes it as the market moves, which is why two banks can approve very different amounts for the same person.

The stress test rate →

Tax settings for investors

Interest on residential rental loans has been fully deductible again since 1 April 2025, and the bright-line test applies to sales within two years. Both change the sums on a rental, so check your own position with an accountant.

Interest deductibility →

Help for first home buyers

The First Home Grant closed on 22 May 2024. The Kāinga Ora First Home Loan remains, letting eligible buyers start from a 5% deposit, and KiwiSaver withdrawals are still available after three years of membership.

Common Questions We Hear

No one can say for certain. Mortgage rates follow the Reserve Bank's Official Cash Rate and wholesale funding costs, and the outlook can change at every OCR review. The Reserve Bank publishes each decision and its forecasts at rbnz.govt.nz. Rather than betting on one outcome, many borrowers split their loan across different fixed terms.

It depends on your plans and how much certainty you want. A shorter term lets you refix sooner if rates fall but exposes you sooner if they rise; a longer term gives certainty for longer. Splitting your loan across terms spreads the risk. We compare current pricing across lenders when your fixed term is due.

Each lender sets its own test (assessment) rate and changes it as market rates move. Lenders check whether you could afford repayments at a rate above the one you'll actually pay, so what you can borrow depends on each lender's current test rate. We compare lenders' current test rates when working out your borrowing power.

Floating rates usually move after an OCR change, but each bank decides how much of the change to pass on and how quickly. Fixed rates are driven more by wholesale swap rates, which move on expectations of future OCR decisions.
