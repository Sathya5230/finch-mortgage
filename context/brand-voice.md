# Finch Mortgages Brand Voice & Messaging

This document defines the Finch Mortgages brand voice, tone, and messaging framework. Reference this when writing all content to ensure consistency.

## Brand Voice Pillars

### 1. Straightforward Adviser
- **What it means**: Mukhtar's own stated approach — "listen first, give honest advice, and support you through every stage" — is the voice for all content.
- **How it sounds**: Plain-English explanations of lending concepts, no hedging, no jargon left unexplained.
- **Example**: "A 20% deposit gets you the best rates, but plenty of our clients buy with 10% or even 5% through First Home Grant and specialist lenders — here's how that works."
- **Avoid**: Corporate bank-speak, vague reassurance ("we'll see what we can do"), salesy pressure.

### 2. Independent & Client-First
- **What it means**: Finch is an independent broker (not tied to one bank), compares 20+ NZ lenders, and charges $0 broker fee to the client (paid by the lender instead).
- **How it sounds**: Framing advice around "what's best for you" vs. "what one bank offers."
- **Example**: "We compare ANZ, ASB, BNZ, Kiwibank, and a dozen non-bank lenders side by side, so the recommendation is based on your situation, not a single bank's book."
- **Avoid**: Implying any one lender is always best; never disparage a specific bank or lender by name.

### 3. Experienced, Not Corporate
- **What it means**: Mukhtar Kiyani has 15+ years of main-bank lending experience across ASB, Westpac, and BNZ — real inside knowledge of how banks assess applications.
- **How it sounds**: Confident, specific, occasionally sharing "how banks actually think" insight.
- **Example**: "Banks stress-test your ability to repay at a rate well above the advertised one — that's why your borrowing power calculator estimate can look lower than expected."
- **Avoid**: Generic "finance influencer" tone; unverifiable claims about rates or approval odds.

### 4. NZ-Specific, Always
- **What it means**: Every guide, calculator, and blog post is written for the New Zealand lending environment — RBNZ rules, LVR restrictions, DTI caps, KiwiSaver, First Home Grant, FSPR regulation.
- **How it sounds**: Uses NZ terminology and regulatory references correctly and specifically.
- **Example**: "Under RBNZ's DTI restrictions, most banks cap lending at 6x gross income for owner-occupiers."
- **Avoid**: US/UK mortgage terminology (e.g. "escrow," "closing costs," "APR" used loosely) — use NZ equivalents.

### 5. Reassuring Without Overpromising
- **What it means**: Many readers are first home buyers, self-employed, or previously declined — anxious audiences. Content should build confidence without guaranteeing outcomes.
- **How it sounds**: Empathetic framing of common worries, followed by concrete next steps.
- **Example**: "A declined application isn't the end of the road — it usually means the file needs repositioning, not that you can't get a mortgage."
- **Avoid**: Promising specific approval, rates, or timelines that depend on individual circumstances.

## Tone Guidelines

### General Tone
Imagine an experienced NZ mortgage adviser who used to work inside the banks, sitting across the table from a client, explaining exactly how the system works and what to do next. Knowledgeable, direct, warm — never salesy, never condescending.

### Tone Variations by Content Type

**How-To Guides / Calculators**: Instructive, step-by-step, reassuring
- "Enter your income and expenses below to get an estimate in under a minute."
- "This is an estimate only — your actual borrowing power depends on the lender's full assessment."
- "Most first home buyers are surprised their borrowing power is higher than they expected."

**Guides & Blog (educational)**: Authoritative, plain-English, example-driven
- "Here's exactly what a bank looks at when assessing a self-employed application."
- "In practice, this means two years of tax returns rather than payslips."
- "The short answer: yes, but the process looks different."

**Weekly / Market Reports**: Analytical, current, NZ-market-specific
- "The OCR cut this week flows through to floating rates within days, fixed rates more slowly."
- "Auckland listings rose 8% month-on-month — more choice for buyers heading into summer."

**Service/Lender Pages**: Benefit-focused, honest, comparison-driven
- "We work with major banks and non-bank lenders — so if your bank says no, that's often not the end of the story."
- "$0 broker fee — Finch is paid by the lender, not by you."

## Writing Style Guidelines

### Sentence Structure
- **Vary length**: Mix short, direct statements with longer explanatory sentences that walk through *why*.
- **Active voice preferred**: "Banks assess your DTI ratio" not "Your DTI ratio is assessed by banks."
- **Average length**: 15-20 words per sentence; break up anything over 25 words.
- **Clarity first**: If a lending concept needs a technical term, define it in-line on first use.

### Paragraph Structure
- **Length**: 2-4 sentences typically.
- **One idea per paragraph**: Especially important in guides — one lending concept per paragraph, not blended.
- **Transitions**: Use natural connectors ("In practice," "That said," "Here's the difference") rather than academic transition words.
- **White space**: Short paragraphs, frequent subheadings — readers are often anxious/skimming for a specific answer (e.g. "how much deposit do I need").

### Word Choice
- **Plain English over jargon**: "how much you can borrow" not "borrowing capacity assessment," unless defining the term.
- **Specific over vague**: "20% deposit" not "a substantial deposit"; "$50 a week" not "significant savings."
- **NZ spelling**: personalise, organise, colour, etc. (not US spelling).
- **Avoid fluff**: Cut "very," "really," "simply," "just" unless load-bearing.

### Terminology

**Say This** → **Not That**
- home loan / mortgage → "loan product" (too vague, sounds like generic finance copy)
- mortgage adviser / broker → "loan officer" (US term, not used in NZ)
- deposit → "down payment" (US term)
- First Home Grant → generic "government grant" (name it specifically)
- pre-approval → "conditional approval" unless quoting a specific lender's term
- refinance / refix → use precisely: refinance = switching lender, refix = new rate with same lender

## Content Formatting

### Headlines
- Be specific and NZ-anchored: "First Home Buyer Guide NZ 2026" not "Home Buying Tips"
- Include the benefit or outcome where possible: "5% Deposit Loans" not just "Low Deposit Options"
- Use numbers/years for freshness signals: "2026", "Step-by-Step"
- Keep under 60 characters for SERP display

### Subheadings
- Every 300-400 words, phrased as the question a reader is actually asking
- Keyword variation included naturally, never forced
- Should make sense read in isolation (skimmers scan headers first)

### Lists
- Use for eligibility criteria, document checklists, step-by-step processes
- Fragments are fine for scannable lists; full sentences for anything with its own logic
- Bold the key term at the start of each bullet when comparing options (lenders, loan types)

### Calls-to-Action
- Primary CTA across the site: "Book a Free Consultation"
- Secondary CTAs: calculator tools ("Estimate your borrowing power"), lender comparison, guide downloads
- Always low-pressure — "no cost, no obligation" framing given $0 broker fee message

## Voice Examples

### Excellent Finch Voice ✅

"Getting declined by your bank doesn't mean you can't get a mortgage — it usually means the file didn't fit that particular bank's current policy. Banks tighten and loosen lending criteria constantly based on their own risk appetite, not just your financial position. We start by figuring out exactly what triggered the decline, then either strengthen the application or move it to a lender whose policy actually fits your situation. For a lot of self-employed clients, that's the whole story: not "your finances are bad," just "wrong bank, wrong month.""

**Why this works**:
- Leads with reassurance grounded in a real mechanism (bank policy, not judgment)
- Uses "we" naturally without being salesy
- Plain English, no unexplained jargon
- Ends on a concrete, memorable insight
- NZ lending context throughout

### Not Finch Voice ❌

"At Finch Mortgages, we are committed to leveraging our extensive network and industry-leading expertise to deliver best-in-class mortgage solutions tailored to your unique financial journey. Our team of dedicated professionals works tirelessly to ensure optimal outcomes for all our valued clients."

**Why this fails**:
- Corporate buzzwords ("leveraging," "best-in-class," "unique financial journey") with no real content
- No specific, verifiable claim — could be any finance business anywhere
- No NZ context
- Doesn't explain anything the reader can actually use

## Audience Understanding

### Who We Write For

**Primary Audience**: NZ home buyers and owners actively navigating a lending decision
- First home buyers researching deposit requirements and KiwiSaver/grant eligibility
- Existing homeowners considering refinancing as fixed rates roll off
- Self-employed applicants unsure if they'll qualify
- Property investors managing DTI/LVR rules and portfolio lending
- People recently declined by a bank, looking for alternatives

**Secondary Audiences**:
- **Next home buyers**: upgrading/moving, often juggling sell-then-buy timing
- **Construction/new-build clients**: progressive drawdown, LVR exemptions
- **Commercial/asset finance clients**: business owners needing equipment or property finance
- **Referral partners**: real estate agents, accountants, lawyers who send clients to Finch

### What They Care About

**Top Priorities**:
1. "Will I actually qualify?" (borrowing power, deposit, credit history)
2. "What will this cost me?" (rates, fees, total repayments)
3. "How long will this take?" (pre-approval and settlement timelines)
4. "What if my bank says no?" (alternatives, non-bank lenders)
5. "Am I getting unbiased advice?" (independence, no hidden fees)

**Pain Points**:
1. Confusing, jargon-heavy bank communication
2. Uncertainty about how much deposit/income is "enough"
3. Fear of being declined and not knowing why
4. Not realising non-bank/specialist lenders are an option
5. Missing out on KiwiSaver/First Home Grant eligibility because they didn't know the rules

### How to Serve Them

- **Respect their time**: Answer the core question in the first 1-2 sentences, then explain.
- **Make it actionable**: Every guide should end with a clear next step (use calculator, book consultation, gather documents).
- **Explain the 'why'**: Don't just state a rule (e.g. DTI cap) — explain why banks apply it and what it means practically.
- **Acknowledge anxiety**: Many readers are stressed about a big financial decision — tone should reduce, not add to, that stress.
- **Be NZ-specific and current**: Reference the current OCR, current LVR/DTI settings, current grant amounts — and flag when something depends on the reader's specific situation.

## Quality Checklist

Before publishing any content, verify:

- [ ] **Voice**: Sounds like an experienced, independent NZ mortgage adviser — not a generic finance blog
- [ ] **Tone**: Reassuring but not overpromising; no guaranteed outcomes
- [ ] **Value**: Answers a real question a NZ borrower is actually asking
- [ ] **Clarity**: Lending/regulatory terms explained on first use
- [ ] **Accuracy**: Rates, rules, and lender details are real and current — never invented
- [ ] **Examples**: NZ-specific scenarios (KiwiSaver, First Home Grant, DTI, LVR)
- [ ] **Action**: Clear next step (calculator, guide, consultation)
- [ ] **Messaging**: Reinforces independence, $0 fee, or real bank experience where relevant
- [ ] **Terminology**: NZ terms used correctly (not US/UK equivalents)
- [ ] **Compliance**: No invented rates, guaranteed approvals, or specific lender promises

**Remember**: Every piece of content should leave a reader feeling like they just got straight answers from someone who used to work inside the banks — not marketing copy. Clarity and honesty are the product.
