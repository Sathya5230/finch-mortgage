Deposits & first homes

Most buyers need 20%. Some lenders accept 10% to 19% and charge a low equity margin, eligible first home buyers can use the Kāinga Ora First Home Loan with 5%, and new builds can need less than 20%. Our deposit guide compares the options, and the LVR calculator shows where you sit.

Usually, yes. After three years as a member you can withdraw your balance apart from $1,000 to buy a home you'll live in. The money is paid to your lawyer, and the application takes a few weeks. See our KiwiSaver first-home withdrawal guide.

No. The First Home Grant closed to new applications in May 2024. KiwiSaver withdrawals and the Kāinga Ora First Home Loan are still available. See what replaced the First Home Grant.

Borrowing

It depends on three things: whether you can afford the repayments at the lender's higher test rate, how your total debt compares with your income, and how much deposit you have. Lenders work these out differently, so the answer varies between them. Read how much can I borrow or try the borrowing power calculator.

Reserve Bank rules limit how much lending banks can do above six times income for owner-occupiers and seven times for investors. New builds are exempt. Check yours with the DTI calculator.

Usually 60 to 90 days, depending on the lender, and it can normally be renewed with updated documents. See mortgage pre-approval.

It depends on the lender and on how complete your documents are. Some decisions come back within days and others take longer, particularly at busy times. Having everything on our document checklist ready is the best way to avoid delays.

Yes. Most lenders want two years of financial statements, and some will consider one year or other evidence of income. See self-employed home loans.

Rates & refinancing

Most borrowers fix most of their loan for certainty and keep a smaller part floating for extra repayments. The right split depends on your plans. See fixed vs floating.

A charge that can apply if you repay or refinance a fixed-rate loan before the term ends and interest rates have fallen since you fixed. Only your bank can give the exact figure. Our break fee calculator gives an estimate.

Possibly. Most cash contributions have to be repaid in full or in part if you leave within a set period, commonly three or four years. Check your loan agreement, or use the cashback clawback calculator.

About two months before a fixed term ends is a good time, because there's no break fee on the expiry date and lenders will often hold a rate for you. See our refinance guide.
