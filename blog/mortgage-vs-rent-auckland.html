Mortgage vs Rent in Auckland 2026: The Real Numbers

Last updated: November 2026

In Auckland, owning a first home usually costs more each week than renting the same place, even before you count the deposit. Part of every mortgage payment builds equity, though, and rent can rise at every review. Which one comes out ahead depends on how long you stay, what prices do, and whether a renter really invests the difference.

By Mukhtar Kiyani — Financial Adviser, Finch Mortgages | Updated November 2026 | Auckland, New Zealand

Compare the right numbers

Most rent-versus-buy maths goes wrong in one of two ways. Put your rent next to the mortgage payment alone and buying looks cheap. Put it next to the full mortgage payment plus every bill and buying looks awful, because part of that payment is your own money going into equity.

The fair comparison is rent against the money you'd lose each week as an owner: interest, council rates, insurance and upkeep. Principal goes in a separate column, because you get it back when you sell.

A worked example

These are assumptions, not quotes. Change them to match a real listing. We've used an $800,000 Auckland townhouse with a 10% deposit ($80,000), a $720,000 loan over 30 years and an interest rate of 5.0% for the whole first year. We've also assumed council rates of $3,500 a year, insurance of $2,400 a year and maintenance of 1% of the property's value. The same townhouse rents for $650 a week.

On those numbers, owning costs about $300 a week more than renting before you count any change in the property's price. You'd also need around $500 a week more in cash, because the $204 of principal still has to be paid each week. That second figure is what your budget has to cover, and it's what a bank tests when it decides whether you can afford the loan.

A 10% deposit usually also means a low-equity margin or fee, which we've left out. Here's how that works.

What the example leaves out

  • Equity from repayments. On the example loan, about $58,800 of principal is paid off over five years even if the price never moves.
  • Rent rises. Your rent can go up at each review, but your mortgage interest drops as the balance shrinks, and you can refix when rates change. How long it takes for those lines to cross depends on rents and rates, and nobody can promise you a date.
  • Price changes cut both ways. Gains apply to the whole $800,000 even though you only put in $80,000. So do losses. A 10% fall wipes out the whole deposit on paper.
  • Buying and selling costs. Legal fees, a building report and moving cost money on the way in, and agent fees cost more on the way out. Over a short stay these can eat most of the equity you've built.

When renting makes more sense

  • You might move within three to five years, for work or family.
  • Buying would empty your savings and leave you nothing for a job loss or a big repair.
  • You'll actually invest the difference. Renting and putting $300 a week into a fund can keep up with owning, but only if the money really goes into the fund every week.

When buying makes more sense

  • You plan to stay at least five years and your income is steady.
  • You can buy and still keep a cash buffer.
  • Your rent is already close to the "money gone" figure for a similar home. Check this for your own suburb, because the gap varies a lot across Auckland.

Frequently asked questions

Is it cheaper to rent or buy in Auckland right now?

Week to week, renting is usually cheaper than the full cost of owning a similar home. Over five to ten years, owning often comes out ahead because your repayments build equity, but that's not guaranteed if prices fall or you sell early.

How much deposit do I need for an $800k Auckland home?

Many first home buyers borrow with 10% ($80k) under banks' low-deposit allowance. A Kāinga Ora First Home Loan can start from 5% if you qualify, and 20% ($160k) usually gets the best rates.

Should I wait for prices or rates to fall before buying?

Timing both perfectly is luck, not strategy. Falling rates tend to support prices, so waiting for cheaper money often means paying more for the house. Buy when your finances and horizon are ready.

Does paying rent count toward getting a mortgage?

A clean rent record shows a lender you can keep up with regular housing costs, and some lenders take it into account. You still have to meet the deposit and income requirements.
