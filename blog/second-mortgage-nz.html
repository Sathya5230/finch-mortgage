Second Mortgages in NZ: What They Are and When They Make Sense

A second mortgage is a separate loan from a different lender, secured behind your main mortgage. It's quick to arrange and expensive. Check whether a top-up or refinance would do the same job for less.

What it is

Your main lender holds the first mortgage over your home. A second mortgage is another lender registering its own mortgage behind that one. If the house were ever sold to repay debts, the first lender is paid in full before the second gets anything.

That's why second mortgages cost more. The second lender carries more risk, so it charges higher interest and fees, usually for a short term of a year or two.

How it compares

Top-up with your lenderRefinance to a new lenderSecond mortgage
Who lendsYour current lenderA new first-mortgage lenderA separate lender, usually non-bank
CostHome loan ratesHome loan rates, plus switching costsMuch higher rates and fees
TermUp to the rest of your loanUp to 30 yearsShort
SpeedDays to weeksWeeksCan be days
Needs first lender's consentIt is the first lenderThe old loan is repaidUsually yes

Start with a top-up or a cash-out refinance. A second mortgage is for when neither is possible.

When people use one

  • A deposit for the next purchase while waiting for a sale.
  • Urgent business cash flow or a tax bill.
  • Your main lender has said no to more lending and breaking the fixed rate to refinance would cost too much.
  • Finishing a renovation or build that's run over budget.

The common thread is that it's short term and there's a clear way to repay it, such as a sale, a refinance once a fixed term ends, or money that's on its way.

The risks

  • Cost. Interest, an establishment fee, legal fees for both sides and sometimes a broker fee. Ask for the total in dollars before you sign.
  • No exit. If the repayment plan falls through, rolling the loan over adds more fees.
  • Your home is on the line twice. Defaulting on the second mortgage can lead to a forced sale even if the first is up to date.
  • Consent. Most first mortgages require the lender's agreement before another mortgage is registered.

Before you sign

  1. Ask your current lender for a top-up, in writing.
  2. Get a break fee quote and price a full refinance.
  3. Write down how and when the second mortgage will be repaid.
  4. Have your own lawyer review the documents.
