Buying a House by Tender or Deadline Sale in NZ

With a tender or deadline sale you make one written offer without knowing what anyone else has bid. You can usually include conditions, which makes them friendlier to buyers with a mortgage than an auction.

How each one works

TenderDeadline saleAuction
How you offerSealed written offer by a set dateWritten offer by a set dateOpen bidding on the day
Can the seller accept early?Usually notOften yesSometimes, with a pre-auction offer
Conditions allowed?YesYesNo
See other offers?NoNoYes
Deposit with offer?Often yesUsually on acceptanceOn the day

The exact rules are in the tender or sale documents, so have your lawyer read them. Some tenders ask for a deposit cheque with the offer, which you get back if you miss out.

Can you include a finance condition?

Yes, and most buyers with a mortgage should unless their lender has already confirmed the property. A conditional offer is weaker than a clean one, though, and sellers can pick any offer they like. They don't have to take the highest.

The stronger move is to do your checks before the deadline. Read the LIM and builder's report, get your lawyer to check the title, and ask us to confirm the property with your lender. Then you can offer with few or no conditions. Here's how the finance condition works if you do keep it.

How to price a blind offer

You only get one shot, so offer the most you'd genuinely pay and no more. A useful test: if you missed out by $5,000, would you be gutted? If so, your number is too low. If you won and felt sick, it's too high.

  • Look at recent sales of similar homes nearby, not asking prices.
  • Check the figure against your pre-approval and your own budget, not just the bank's limit.
  • Remember the lender lends against its valuation. If you pay well over that, you cover the gap in cash.

Getting your mortgage ready

Start a couple of weeks before the deadline. Lenders can take time to assess a property, and a registered valuation takes days to arrange.
