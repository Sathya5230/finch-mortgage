First Home Buyer Guide
New Zealand 2026

12 min read Updated April 2026 Finch Mortgages Team

Where to Start

Buying your first home in New Zealand can feel overwhelming. This guide breaks the process into manageable steps, covering everything from KiwiSaver to settlement day. Let's start with the most important question:

Can you afford to buy? The answer depends on your income, existing savings, KiwiSaver balance, and monthly expenses. Use the Finch Borrowing Power Calculator or book a free consultation to find out exactly where you stand.

Deposit Requirements

The standard requirement for first home buyers in NZ is 20% deposit. However, there are several ways to buy with less:

  • Welcome Home Loan (Kāinga Ora): Buy with 5% deposit if you meet income and price caps
  • KiwiSaver First Home Withdrawal: Access most of your balance after 3+ years of contributions
  • First Home Grant: Up to $10,000 grant on top of your KiwiSaver withdrawal
  • Family Guarantee: Use a parent's home equity to supplement your deposit
  • Specialist lenders: Some non-bank lenders offer loans at 10–15% deposit

KiwiSaver for First Home Buyers

KiwiSaver is one of the most powerful tools for first home buyers in NZ. If you've been contributing for at least 3 years, you can withdraw most of your balance towards a home deposit.

Important: You must leave a minimum $1,000 in your KiwiSaver account. The withdrawal must be used on the same day as settlement — your solicitor handles this. Finch coordinates the process with your KiwiSaver provider.

First Home Grant

The Kāinga Ora First Home Grant tops up your deposit on top of your KiwiSaver withdrawal:

  • Existing home: $1,000 per year of KiwiSaver contributions, up to $5,000
  • New build: $2,000 per year, up to $10,000
  • Eligibility: Income caps apply ($95K single, $150K combined) and house price caps by region

Getting Pre-Approved

Pre-approval is a conditional commitment from a lender to lend you up to a specified amount. It's essential before you start property hunting — especially for auctions where the sale is unconditional.

Finch applies to the most suitable lender for your situation (not just the cheapest advertised rate), maximising your approval odds and securing the best terms.

Other Costs to Budget For

Beyond the deposit, first home buyers should budget for:

  • Legal/conveyancing fees: $1,500–$3,000
  • Building inspection: $400–$700
  • LIM report: $200–$400
  • Registered valuation: $700–$1,200 (usually required by lender)
  • Moving costs: $500–$3,000 depending on distance

The Complete 2026 NZ First Home Buyer Guide

Buying your first home in New Zealand is the single largest financial decision most Kiwis make, and the playbook in 2026 looks quite different from a decade ago. House prices remain elevated relative to incomes, the Credit Contracts and Consumer Finance Act (CCCFA) scrutinises every dollar of your living expenses, and the Reserve Bank of New Zealand's LVR speed-limit framework caps how much your bank can lend against your deposit. The good news: KiwiSaver balances are healthier than ever, Kāinga Ora support is wider, and an independent broker who knows every lender's policy can move you from "first explore" to "settled and in the front door" with far less friction than going straight to your own bank.

Step 1: Understand Your Deposit Mix

Your "deposit" doesn't have to come from cash savings alone. NZ first home buyers routinely combine multiple sources:

  • KiwiSaver withdrawal — after 3 years of contributions, with $1,000 retained.
  • Cash savings — evidenced via regular deposit history (banks like to see discipline, not just a balance).
  • Kāinga Ora First Home Grant — up to $5,000 (existing home) or $10,000 (new build) per applicant.
  • Gifted deposit — most NZ lenders accept a family gift evidenced by a signed gift letter.
  • Family Guarantee — a parent or family member uses equity in their property to cover a deposit shortfall.
  • Kāinga Ora First Home Loan — buy with just 5% deposit through Westpac, Kiwibank, SBS, The Co-operative Bank.

Step 2: Get Match-Ready with the Right NZ Lender

Each of ANZ, ASB, BNZ, Westpac, Kiwibank, TSB, SBS, The Co-operative Bank, and Heartland runs its own first-home-buyer scorecard. ANZ and ASB tend to be efficient on clean PAYE applications; BNZ and Westpac are often generous with professional buyers; Kiwibank, SBS, and The Co-operative Bank are strongest on Welcome Home / First Home Loan low-deposit cases. Specialist non-bank lenders (Resimac, Pepper Money, Avanti Finance, Liberty Financial) step in when you have variable income, a recent business start, or a credit history blemish. See our full NZ lender directory.

Step 3: Pre-Approval Before You House-Hunt

A pre-approval is conditional on a satisfactory valuation, a clean property report, and the property being on the lender's acceptable list. NZ first-home buyers should hold an unconditional or near-unconditional pre-approval before attending any auction. Read more in our NZ pre-approval service page.

Step 4: Bid, Negotiate & Sign Up Smart

In Auckland, Wellington, Tauranga and Christchurch, most properties sell at auction — meaning unconditional bidding. In slower regions, you can usually negotiate by private treaty with finance, building report, and LIM conditions attached. Finch helps you decide which conditions matter for your circumstances and how to word them so vendors take you seriously.

Step 5: Settlement and Beyond

From an accepted offer in NZ, settlement is typically 4–6 weeks. Your solicitor handles legal transfer, your lender drops the funds, you collect the keys. Then your real journey begins: structure your loan with a sensible mix of fixed and floating, plan extra repayments, and review annually.
