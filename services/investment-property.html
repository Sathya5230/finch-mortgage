How lenders assess investment property

Buying a rental is assessed differently from buying a home. The deposit is bigger, less of the income counts, and the way the lending is set up affects your tax position for years.

Deposit and equity

The Reserve Bank's loan-to-value rules mean most lenders want around 30% for an existing rental. New builds are exempt and can need less. Most investors don't use cash. They borrow against equity in their own home, which is why a current valuation is often the first step. See using home equity to invest and releasing equity.

Income and debt-to-income limits

Lenders count only part of the rent, to allow for vacancies and costs, and they test the loan at a rate above the real one. Banks are also restricted in lending to investors above seven times income. Investors with several properties usually hit the income test before they run out of equity.

Structure and tax

From 1 April 2025, interest on residential rental borrowing is fully deductible again, according to Inland Revenue. Deductibility follows what the money was used for, so keep rental borrowing in its own loan and never mix it with private spending. Many investors run the rental loan interest-only and pay down their own home first. The bright-line test still applies to sales within two years. We arrange lending, not tax advice, so confirm the tax side with your accountant. Background in is mortgage interest deductible?.

The property itself

Rentals must meet the Healthy Homes standards. Small apartments, leasehold titles and short-stay properties are restricted by many lenders. We don't give advice on which property or area to buy. We can tell you how a lender will see the one you've found.

For residential home loans our advice costs you nothing. The lender pays us when the loan settles, and we disclose that in writing.
