How asset finance works

Asset finance pays for vehicles, machinery and equipment, with the item itself as security for the loan. Your cash stays in the business, and you don't need to mortgage your house to buy a truck.

The main types

With a secured loan, sometimes called a chattel mortgage, you own the asset from the start and the lender registers a security interest over it. With a finance lease, the lender owns it and you pay to use it, usually with the option to buy at the end. With an operating lease, you rent it and hand it back. Each is treated differently for tax and GST, so check with your accountant which suits you. Our article on chattel mortgages, leases and hire purchase compares them.

What lenders look at

The asset: its age, condition and how easily it could be resold. Common vehicles and equipment are the easiest to finance. Very specialised machinery has fewer willing lenders. And the business: how long it has traded, its accounts or bank statements, and its credit history. New businesses can still be financed, often with a larger deposit or a director's guarantee.

Terms

Terms usually run from two to five years, and shouldn't be longer than the working life of the asset. Some loans have a larger final payment, which lowers the regular ones and needs planning for. Rates depend on the asset, the term and the strength of the business, so we don't quote them here.

Before you sign with the dealer

Finance offered at the point of sale is convenient, and it isn't always the cheapest. Compare the total cost over the whole term, including fees and any final payment. If you also have a home loan application coming up, remember that vehicle finance reduces what you can borrow.

We'll tell you about any fee before you commit to anything. Commission paid by the lender is disclosed in writing.
